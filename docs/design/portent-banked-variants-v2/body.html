Banked foretelling rolls — die-face variants, critically examined

Round 2. The chip idiom of round 1 is demoted: a chip is a label, and Portent's values are physical dice — so the bank now draws outlined d20 faces with the rolled number inside (glyph: d20 dice by Michelle Lukezic, Noun Project, CC-BY 3.0 — attributed in CREDITS.md). Click a face to type the roll by hand; the empty face is the entry point. Marking a face spent draws the sheet's own X-cross over the whole glyph. All panels are the Passive-tab row at the 620px default tier, both states shown.

Each variant now names what is wrong with it. A design that cannot say its failure mode is not ready to ship.

1 · Faces beside the pips — the direct translation

Two banked faces inline before the pip track: d20 label, Roll 2d20 pill, the two faces, then the shipped tracker. Click a face value → inline edit (the shipped numberField dance). Click a face frame while a value sits in it → spent: X across the glyph, one pip checks.

Critique. Two d20 glyphs now sit in one detail slot — the serif d20 label and the face glyphs — and a reader who does not know the feature must guess which is which. The 40px faces (twice the pips' size) dominate a row whose shipped hierarchy puts the count first. And the row is getting long: label, pill, two faces, two pips, caption — five clusters where the shipped row has two. It works, but it is the least economical reading.

2 · Faces replace the pips — the strongest claim, restated

If the faces carry both value and spent-state, the pips are redundant — every face is its own pip. The tracker reduces to the faces plus the / Long Rest caption. The count the engine tracks (max − used) is still the single source; it just has no widget of its own, exactly like a charge bar's zones carry no numbers.

Critique. This is the honest shape of the feature — the faces ARE the state — but it breaks the sheet's contract: every other resource on this tab draws the shipped tracker, and Portent would be the one row with a bespoke widget. A player who never rolls d20s at all (all manual, empty faces) sees two ghost dice where the rest of the sheet shows countable boxes. It is also the variant that leans hardest on the X: at a glance, a spent face and a faded face are one opacity decision apart.

3 · The faces live in the expand card — the quiet option

Row unchanged from shipped. The card gains a Foretelling rolls line: the pill, then the faces, in the card's resource-line register. The faces are 32px here (card context, not row context) — proportionally what the shipped die label reads at.

Critique. Nothing new on the row, the values live beside the rule text that governs them. But the whole point of Portent is that the values are needed mid-combat, someone else's turn — "before the roll" — and burying them one click deep is the least available placement. The card is the right home for prose, not for state you read under time pressure.

4 · Faces as the pip track — the merged widget

What if the faces are the charge boxes? The pip row is replaced one-for-one by faces: same slot, same size rhythm as the toggle boxes. Empty unspent face → ghost die with the entry ring. This is the only variant where the shipped tracker's position survives unchanged — the widget inside it changes.

Critique. The tightest composition — one widget where V1 had three — and it keeps the tracker slot. But 24px is small: a two-digit serif number inside a 24px outlined die is at the floor of legibility, and the X-cross at that scale competes with the number it crosses out. If the table plays on a laptop at 100% zoom this is the one to beat; if anyone is on a small window, V1's 40px is the safer size and the extra clusters are the price.
